DISTRICTS OF KERALA

Kollam

Overview:

  • Location: Kollam is a coastal city in the southern part of Kerala, located along the Laccadive Sea and known for its historical significance and scenic beauty.
  • Historical Significance:
    • Ancient Port City: Historically known as Quilon, Kollam was a major port for international trade, especially in spices, cashews, and coir.
    • Cultural Heritage: The city reflects Portuguese, Dutch, and British influences in its architecture and cultural traditions.
  • Tourist Attractions:
    • Ashtamudi Lake: Known as the gateway to Kerala's backwaters, it is popular for houseboat cruises, offering beautiful views of the surrounding landscapes.
    • Thangassery Lighthouse: A historic lighthouse providing panoramic views of the Arabian Sea, located near Portuguese and Dutch fort remnants.
    • Jatayu Earth’s Center: A unique rock-themed park with the world's largest bird sculpture, representing the mythical Jatayu from the Ramayana.
  • Natural Beauty:
    • Beaches: Kollam is home to pristine beaches like Kollam Beach and Thirumullavaram Beach, ideal for relaxation and coastal views.
    • Thenmala: India’s first planned eco-tourism destination, offering adventure, culture, and nature activities.
  • Economy:
    • Cashew Industry: Known as the “Cashew Capital of the World,” Kollam is a leading producer and exporter of cashews.
    • Coir and Marine Products: Kollam has a thriving coir industry and is a major producer of marine products.
  • Cultural Significance:
    • Festivals: Kollam celebrates festivals like Kollam Pooram at Asramam Maidan, featuring traditional art forms.
    • Art and Craft: Known for its traditional art forms, including Kathakali and classical music.
  • Educational Institutions:
    • TKM College of Engineering: One of Kerala's oldest engineering colleges, contributing to regional education.
    • Fisheries College and Research Institute: A key institution for marine studies and research.
  • Transport and Connectivity:
    • Kollam Railway Station: A major railway junction connecting Kollam to other parts of Kerala and India.
    • Waterways: Well-connected by inland waterways, supporting trade and transportation.
  • Tourism and Economy: Kollam's economy thrives on cashew processing, coir products, tourism, and fishing, alongside a blend of cultural and natural attractions.

Kollam is a city that beautifully combines historical legacy with natural beauty, making it a unique destination in Kerala.
