Explore the Rich Culture of Kerala

Discover Kerala's vibrant culture through its traditional art, festivals, cuisine, and more.

Kathakali Dance

Kathakali is a classical dance-drama originating from Kerala, India, known for its elaborate costumes, intricate makeup, and captivating storytelling. This art form, which dates back to the 17th century, combines drama, dance, music, and ritual to depict ancient tales from Hindu epics like the Ramayana and Mahabharata. Kathakali performers use highly stylized gestures, facial expressions, and body movements to convey emotions and narrative, bringing mythical characters to life in vivid detail. The characters are often distinguished by their distinct, colorful makeup and costumes, each representing specific traits or moral qualities. Traditionally performed in temple festivals and cultural events, Kathakali showcases Kerala's rich cultural heritage and remains one of the most iconic representations of Indian classical arts on the world stage.

Traditional Temples

Kerala's traditional temples are celebrated for their unique architectural style, spiritual significance, and historical value, embodying the region's rich cultural heritage. Unlike the towering, ornate structures found in other parts of India, Kerala temples are typically built with sloping, tiled roofs suited to the region's heavy monsoons. The temple architecture emphasizes simplicity and harmony with nature, often incorporating wood, stone, and brass details that enhance their timeless beauty. Each temple usually includes a sanctum sanctorum (garbhagriha), a namaskara mandapam for prayers, and a chuttambalam (enclosed pathway), offering a peaceful atmosphere for worship and meditation. Known for intricate woodwork and exquisite murals depicting mythological stories, many Kerala temples also host annual festivals featuring vibrant processions, traditional music, and dance performances. These temples, integral to local communities, reflect Kerala’s enduring devotion and serve as a testament to the state's cultural and spiritual legacy.

Onam Festival

Onam is Kerala’s most vibrant and widely celebrated festival, marking the annual harvest and the return of the mythical King Mahabali, whose reign is remembered as a golden era of prosperity, equality, and joy. Celebrated over ten days in the Malayalam month of Chingam (August-September), Onam is a time of family gatherings, grand feasts, and colorful festivities that bring people together across the state. The highlight of Onam celebrations is the Onasadya, a lavish banquet served on banana leaves, featuring an array of traditional vegetarian dishes like sambar, avial, and payasam. Throughout the festival, homes are adorned with pookkalam (floral rangolis), and communities participate in traditional games and cultural events such as vallamkali (snake boat races), kaikottikali (a graceful women’s dance), and pulikali (tiger dance). Onam embodies Kerala’s cultural richness, agricultural heritage, and spirit of unity, welcoming the spirit of King Mahabali and celebrating the abundance of the harvest season.

Kerala Sadhya

The Onam Sadhya is a grand vegetarian feast served during the Onam festival in Kerala, known for its diversity of flavors, colors, and textures. Traditionally served on a banana leaf, this meal consists of a variety of dishes that highlight Kerala’s culinary heritage. A typical sadhya includes rice as the main course, accompanied by a wide array of side dishes, curries, pickles, and desserts. Key dishes include avial (a thick mix of vegetables in a coconut and yogurt base), thoran (stir-fried vegetables with coconut), kootu curry (a dish of cooked vegetables and black chickpeas), olan (a mild stew of ash gourd and cowpeas in coconut milk), and sambar (a tangy lentil-based curry with vegetables).

Other essentials are pachadi (a yogurt-based side dish), inji puli (a tangy ginger-tamarind relish), kichadi (a cucumber-yogurt mixture), and various pickles. No sadhya is complete without the famous papadum (crisp lentil crackers), banana chips, and sharkara varatti (sweet fried banana chips coated with jaggery). The meal ends on a sweet note with multiple varieties of payasam (sweet pudding), like palada and parippu payasam, making the Onam Sadhya a sumptuous and memorable culinary experience. This traditional feast reflects Kerala’s agricultural abundance and the joy of togetherness during the Onam festival.
